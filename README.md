# toric-ideals

Authors: Formal Frontier Agents. Original Formal Frontier contributions are
licensed under Apache-2.0; see the complete root `LICENSE` file. The project
uses AI-assisted formalization and review; mathematical results, code, and
attribution still require independent checks before release.

Reusable Lean 4 theory of monomial maps and the Hankel ideal of the rational
normal curve over arbitrary commutative coefficient rings.

## Headline results

- **Coefficientwise fiber normalization:** When *every* source monomial and
  coefficient is congruent modulo an ideal to a representative chosen by its
  target exponent, an element killed by the induced monomial map belongs to
  that ideal. The [generic theorem](ToricIdeals/MonoidAlgebra/FiberNormalization.lean#L42)
  works for a commutative coefficient ring and additive commutative exponent
  monoids in independent universes; the choice of representative need not be
  additive or a section. This proves kernel containment, not injectivity.
- **Hankel presentation of a rational normal curve:** For `1 ≤ n`, the
  parametrization `xᵢ ↦ a^(n-i)b^i` has kernel exactly the ideal generated by
  the `2 × 2` Hankel minors, over *any* commutative coefficient ring
  ([`hankelIdeal_eq_ker`](ToricIdeals/RationalNormalCurve.lean#L707)).
  At `n = 0` the inclusion is strict when the ring is nontrivial, while a
  stored subsingleton client gives equality; at `n = 1` the map is injective
  without a field or domain hypothesis. The [curve module](ToricIdeals/RationalNormalCurve.lean)
  develops the decreasing-energy reduction and uniqueness *among terminal
  exponents*; its choices are classical, not executable canonical algorithms.
- **Quotient image and domain consequences:** At positive degree,
  [`quotientEquivRange`](ToricIdeals/RationalNormalCurve.lean#L715) identifies
  the quotient by the Hankel ideal with the **image subring** of the monomial
  map, not the whole target ring. With the *additional* assumption `[IsDomain k]`,
  [`hankelIdeal_isPrime`](ToricIdeals/RationalNormalCurve.lean#L721),
  [`quotient_isDomain`](ToricIdeals/RationalNormalCurve.lean#L727) and
  [`spectrum_irreducible`](ToricIdeals/RationalNormalCurve.lean#L734) give the
  prime-ideal, quotient-domain and irreducible-prime-spectrum consequences.

This library does not classify general toric ideals or prove rational-normal-curve
results outside the stated parametrization and hypotheses. It is organized for
reuse independently of its source motivation; source-passage correspondence and
coverage are tracked separately and not claimed by this README.

## Mathematical API

All names below are public. The generic theorem is in namespace `ToricIdeals`;
the curve names are in `ToricIdeals.RationalNormalCurve`. Both leaf imports are
also re-exported by `ToricIdeals`.

- **Generic fibers:** `AddMonoidAlgebra.mem_ideal_of_mapDomain_eq_zero` says that
  if, for *every* source exponent `m` and coefficient `r`, its basis term is
  congruent modulo `I` to the term at `normal (exponentMap m)`, then anything
  mapped to zero by `AddMonoidAlgebra.mapDomain exponentMap` lies in `I`.
  It works for a commutative coefficient ring and additive commutative source
  and target monoids in independent universes. `normal` is an arbitrary function,
  not necessarily additive or a section; no injectivity or finiteness is assumed.
- **Parametrization and generators:** `Source`, `Target`,
  `coordinateExponent`, `rncExponent`, `rncMap`, `MinorIndex`, `hankelMinor`,
  `hankelIdeal` give the `n+1` source variables, two target variables, the map
  `xᵢ ↦ a^(n-i)b^i`, ordered column pairs `i < j` in the `2 × n` Hankel matrix,
  and the ideal generated by its `2 × 2` minors. `rncExponent_single`,
  `rncMap_X`, `minor_exponents_agree`, `minor_mem`, `hankelIdeal_le_ker` give
  basis images, the generating relation and the inclusion valid for *all* `n`.
- **Small degrees:** `hankelIdeal_zero`, `hankelIdeal_one` show that both
  minor ideals are zero. `hankelIdeal_zero_lt_ker` needs `[Nontrivial k]`:
  at `n = 0` the sole variable maps to `1`, so the inclusion is strict.
  For `[Subsingleton k]`, the stored root-import client instead proves equality
  by subsingleton elimination. At `n = 1`, `rncExponent_one`,
  `rncMap_one_injective`, `hankelIdeal_one_eq_ker` give an injective map and
  equality without an extra hypothesis on `k`.
- **Decreasing reduction:** `sourceDegree`, `sourceWeight`, `sourceEnergy`,
  `outerExponent`, `innerExponent`, `reduceExponent` describe a move from a
  separated occupied pair toward adjacent positions. `monomial_add_one`,
  `monomial_outer_sub_inner`, `rncExponent_outer_eq_inner`,
  `inner_energy_lt_outer_energy`, `reduceExponent_preserves_rncExponent`,
  `reduceExponent_energy_lt`, `monomial_sub_reduceExponent_mem` establish that
  each applicable move preserves the target exponent, strictly decreases a
  natural-number energy and changes the monomial only modulo `hankelIdeal`.
- **Terminal exponents:** `Normal` forbids applicable separated pairs.
  `Normal.index_le_add_one`, `Normal.mem_support_eq_or_val_eq_add_one`,
  `Normal.degree_weight_eq_min_add_succ`,
  `Normal.eq_single_add_single_succ`, `Normal.eq_single_of_min_eq_last`,
  `Normal.min_val_eq_weight_div_degree`, `Normal.eq_of_degree_weight_eq`
  establish adjacent support and uniqueness from degree and weighted index.
  `exists_normal` obtains a terminal representative by energy induction.
- **Target invariants and choices:** `rncExponent_second`, `rncExponent_total`
  identify the second target exponent with source weight and the sum of the
  two target exponents with `n * sourceDegree`. Hence
  `rncExponent_injective_on_normal` recovers the degree for `1 ≤ n` and proves
  injectivity *on terminal exponents*, not on all source exponents.
  `normalSourceExponent`, `normalSourceExponent_normal`,
  `normalSourceExponent_rncExponent`,
  `monomial_sub_normalSourceExponent_mem`,
  `normalSourceExponent_eq_of_rncExponent_eq`, `fiberNormalExponent`,
  `fiberNormalExponent_image` select congruent terminal/fiber representatives.
  These selections use classical noncomputable choice (including a coefficient
  ring parameter); they are not an executable or universally canonical normal
  form. By definition `fiberNormalExponent` returns zero outside the image.
- **Kernel and geometry:** `ker_le_hankelIdeal` and `hankelIdeal_eq_ker`
  give the reverse inclusion and equality for `1 ≤ n` over any `[CommRing k]`.
  `quotientEquivRange` identifies `Source k n ⧸ hankelIdeal k n` with the
  **image subring** `(rncMap k n).range`, not all of `Target k`. In general its
  dependent transport does not provide an `rfl`/`simp` evaluation on arbitrary
  quotient representatives; the unit evaluation in the stored client uses
  `map_one`. With `[IsDomain k]`, `hankelIdeal_isPrime`, `quotient_isDomain`,
  `spectrum_irreducible` give the prime, quotient-domain and topological
  consequences. No algebraic-closedness, characteristic, smoothness,
  scheme-isomorphism or general toric-ideal classification is asserted.

## Use

Import `ToricIdeals` for both public APIs, or import either leaf module directly.
The following complete native module is stored verbatim in
[`ToricIdealsTest/ReadmeKernel.lean`](ToricIdealsTest/ReadmeKernel.lean) and built
by the default test target:

```lean
/-
SPDX-License-Identifier: Apache-2.0
Authors: Formal Frontier Agents
-/
module

import ToricIdeals.RationalNormalCurve

/-! # README example: the positive-degree kernel through a direct leaf import -/

open ToricIdeals.RationalNormalCurve

universe u

private theorem readme_degree_two_kernel (k : Type u) [CommRing k] :
    hankelIdeal k 2 = RingHom.ker (rncMap k 2) :=
  hankelIdeal_eq_ker k 2 (by decide)
```

`ToricIdealsTest.RootClient` stores further independent-universe generic,
low-degree, quotient/range and domain/spectrum examples using just the root import;
`ToricIdealsTest.Audit` stores named boundary clients and selected axiom prints.
These are stored tests, not extra library declarations. Leaf-module users can
import `ToricIdeals.MonoidAlgebra.FiberNormalization` or
`ToricIdeals.RationalNormalCurve` directly. Keep the `1 ≤ n` and `[IsDomain k]`
assumptions when using the corresponding results.

## Build

Use the repository's `lean-toolchain` and pinned `lake-manifest.json`. Fetch the
matching precompiled mathlib cache before building:

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build --wfail
```

The literal `lake build --wfail` builds both the named `ToricIdeals` library and
the `ToricIdealsTest` target, including all three stored test modules. For a
focused check instead, use `lake build ToricIdeals --wfail` or
`lake build ToricIdealsTest --wfail` after fetching the same cache; neither is
an additional mandatory stage after the default build. A successful build and
the example imports do not substitute for an applicable complete transitive
standard-axiom audit (including private declarations), independent review or
release acceptance. Existing unchanged-input evidence may be reused; this
README does not claim a new candidate check. See the historical
[complete public API reference](docs/API.md), [exact input manifest](docs/api-manifest.json)
and [native documentation reproduction instructions](docs/README.md).
The reference retains all public signatures and docstrings and the six modules'
documentation. It ships Markdown only, not an interactive dependency website.
Formal Frontier agents developed and independently reviewed the integrated
mathematical code; release review binds the complete artifact to an exact
commit/tree and is recorded separately from this timeless package description.
No human-review, benchmark-comparison or source-coverage claim is made.

## Observed build baseline

One serial run on Linux with Lean `v4.34.0-rc2` and the committed dependency
manifest fetched the matching mathlib cache successfully before compiling. The
cache step, including first dependency setup and cache-tool compilation, took
170.38 seconds. Clean builds of the six project modules totaled 35.76 seconds;
their separate warning-fatal source checks totaled 28.45 seconds. Subsequent
named-library/test and default builds took 3.64 and 3.58 seconds. Dependencies
were cached, not freshly rebuilt from source.

The maximum observed child-process resident set for those project build/source
commands was 1,476,904 KiB (`getrusage(RUSAGE_CHILDREN).ru_maxrss`), not a sampled
aggregate Pod-memory peak. Timings are a reproducible-environment observation,
not a performance guarantee, cross-library comparison or release certificate.
